01 / Why now?

A different moment for digital ownership.

Digital possessions are part of everyday life. Earlier approaches to digital ownership faced technical, economic and legal constraints. We believe a more digitally fluent society and more accessible research tools make this question worth revisiting.

Society

People routinely buy digital goods, manage online identities and make electronic transactions. Meaningful digital ownership is easier to imagine in that context.

Technology

Modern cryptography, distributed systems and portable runtimes provide building blocks for content that can be verified independently of its seller.

AI-assisted research

AI helps independent engineers explore literature, connect disciplines, prototype and challenge complex designs with fewer resources. It assists the research; it is not the ownership authority.

This is a research opportunity, not a claim that digital ownership has never been attempted. Rights, economics, usability and independent security validation still matter.

02 / How it works

Encryption, integrity and verifiable ownership.

The idea is simple: a portable encrypted package carries verifiable evidence of authenticity and historical ownership, alongside purchaser-controlled keys. Instead of asking a retailer to authorize every use, compatible software can check the evidence.

Encrypted content

Protect the media from unauthorized access. Possession of the encrypted file alone does not confer the authority to decrypt it.

Tamper-evident by design

Digital content should carry evidence of its authenticity, not simply rely on its distributor. Cryptographic signatures, authenticated encryption and hash-linked records allow conforming verifiers to detect invalid changes to protected content, authenticated metadata and ownership evidence.

Compact blockchain evidence

Bundle signed issuance records and compact proofs from a purpose-built, hash-linked ownership ledger. The package does not contain the entire live chain; fresh canonical title and transfers require verified ledger finality.

Ownership ledger specification

Owner-controlled authority

Keep recovery and ordinary device authorization under the purchaser’s control. Legitimate playback is designed to work offline; copying the encrypted package alone does not grant playback authority.

Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. A package can be altered, but invalid changes to authenticated data should fail verification against trusted evidence. Legitimate ownership updates remain possible; verification does not prevent copying, destruction or plaintext capture.

CONTENTPKG explores reusable packaging and cryptographic mechanics; each specialization defines its own ownership, access and transfer rules. MUSICPKG is the first working draft; other content profiles are research directions. CONTENTPKG is not a published general-purpose standard or a commercial rights service.

04 / Engineering and research

Open contracts. Independent implementations.

The published MUSICPKG working draft is intended to let independent implementers build interoperable producers, verifiers and players without a private repository or Micrantha-operated service.

Applied cryptography

Authenticated media, signed issuance, owner-controlled recovery and explicit trust boundaries.

Protocol specifications

Interoperability

Deterministic schemas, test vectors and a public Rust reference conformance harness.

Public test vectors

Security and correctness

Separate historical evidence from current ownership, and state what protection can and cannot guarantee.

Security model
Research status. MUSICPKG v0.2 is a working draft. The public corpus contains 26 executable reference checks and 23 contract assertions, not a complete product-verification suite. Full end-to-end production verification and ownership infrastructure are not established.
